# ORBITAL — Synthetic Crew & Scenarios

> **Every value in this build is SYNTHETIC.** The five crew members are fictional. Their data are generated by `@orbital/simulation` (`sim.v1.0`) and are **not** NASA astronaut measurements. Nothing here is a clinical reference range or a diagnosis.

## 1. The five profiles

| ID | Name (fictional) | Role | Scenario | Ground truth (simulation forcing) | Brain Health engine result (tested) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| CM-01 | Alex Morgan | Commander | **Healthy adaptation** | Early adaptation only (first ~2 weeks) | NO on every assessable day (44–179) |
| CM-02 | Maya Chen | Flight Engineer | **Sleep disruption** | Ramps from day 45 to full by day 95. Recovery intervention on day 120 decays it to about 35%. | First YES about day 59 (sleep, cognition). NO windows reappear after the intervention (partial recovery). |
| CM-03 | Daniel Reed | Mission Specialist | **Physical deconditioning** | Exercise adherence declines from day 55 to full by day 165 | NO throughout. The changes appear in Physical Health (resting HR ↑, exercise capacity ↓, leg lean mass ↓), not as a wellbeing signal. |
| CM-04 | Sofia Patel | Payload Specialist | **Wellbeing changes** | Multi-domain forcing ramps from day 85 to full by day 140 and persists | NO before onset. First YES about day 106, then persistent. Up to 6 domains contribute. |
| CM-05 | James Wilson | Flight Engineer | **Incomplete telemetry** | No physiological forcing. Data availability and quality are degraded instead. | Mostly INSUFFICIENT DATA (more than 70% of assessable days), with brief NO windows. Never YES. |

The engine results are asserted in `tests/integration/crew-scenarios.test.ts`. The about-21-day latency for Sofia is by design: the monitor reports *sustained* change (persistence, EWMA and CUSUM must all agree), not a single bad day.

**Every profile has a different personal baseline:** resting HR 50–61 bpm, HRV 47–76 ms, sleep 6.9–7.5 h, body mass 61–86 kg, reaction time 255–281 ms, and so on. See `packages/simulation/src/profiles.ts`.

## 2. How the data are generated

### Stage 1: latent model (the person's true state)
Each day, four hidden drivers evolve as AR(1) processes with unit stationary variance: sleep variability, stress load, mood and activity. They combine with the scenario forcing functions (`scenarios.ts`) and with a running **sleep-debt** accumulator:

```
debt_t = 0.6 · debt_{t−1} + max(0, baselineSleep − sleep_t)
```

Signals share drivers, so correlations arise by construction rather than by coincidence. These relationships are tested on the true (latent) values:
- Sleep debt → higher fatigue, slower reaction time and lower HRV over the following days (|r| > 0.4–0.5).
- Wellbeing forcing → lower mood and motivation, higher stress and isolation, lower sleep quality, slower reaction time and lower memory accuracy. Mood and stress are negatively correlated (r < −0.6).
- Deconditioning → lower activity and exercise adherence, lower exercise capacity, higher resting HR, lower HRV, and lean-mass loss concentrated in the legs.
- Everyone gets a small weekly rhythm (weekends), a scheduled rest day every 7th day (exercise = 0, which is planned rest, not non-compliance), a mild mission-long rise in perceived isolation, and early adaptation to microgravity.

### Stage 2: observation model (what the instruments captured)
| Mechanism | How it's modelled |
|---|---|
| Wear compliance | Per-day probability that wearable data exist (CM-05: 0.76, plus reduced availability in days 0–40) |
| Multi-day outages | CM-05: 6 device outages of 4–12 days |
| Check-in compliance | Per-day probability the morning check-in and cognitive tasks were done. Individual items are sometimes skipped. |
| Measurement noise | Per-signal SD × profile multiplier (CM-05: ×1.8) |
| Motion artefacts / poor contact | Flagged readings with low quality scores (0.2–0.5) and extra noise |
| Gross errors | CM-05 only: values outside physical plausibility, *not* flagged by the "device". The engine's data-quality rules must catch them. |
| Rounding | Self-reports are integers 0–10. Units follow the signal registry. |
| Inferred indices | `fatigue.index` and `hydration.index` are computed only from *observed, reliable* values on the same day and classified **inferred**. |

### Cadence
| Cadence | Signals |
|---|---|
| Daily / nightly (wearable) | Heart rate (daily mean), resting HR, HRV (RMSSD), nightly SpO₂, respiratory rate, skin temperature, sleep duration and efficiency, active minutes |
| Daily (check-in / tasks) | Sleep quality, fatigue, mood, stress, motivation, perceived isolation, concentration, fluid intake, reaction time, lapses, memory accuracy, exercise logs |
| Weekly (scheduled, staggered per person) | Blood pressure, spot SpO₂, fasting glucose, body mass (inertial device), exercise capacity (ergometer test) |
| Monthly (days 0, 30, … 150, 179) | Bioimpedance: fat-free mass, fat mass, muscle, skeletal muscle, body water, segmental lean mass |

### Telemetry playback
Minute-level traces (2-minute resolution) for the selected day are generated lazily from `(seed, day)`. They're anchored to that day's daily records: resting HR, sleep duration, scheduled exercise minutes, and whether the device was worn. The UI labels them **"Simulation playback — not real-time sensor telemetry"**.

## 3. Assumptions and simplifications (stated, not hidden)
1. **Baselines are simulation parameters**, plausible for healthy adults. They are not reference ranges and are never shown as "normal".
2. **Lean-mass loss:** about 2% of fat-free mass over 180 days with exercise, plus up to 7.5% extra with deconditioning. Legs are weighted ×1.8 and arms ×0.6, reflecting lower-limb unloading in microgravity. Illustrative magnitudes only.
3. **Bioimpedance in flight** includes a +0.8% bias after launch to represent fluid-shift effects, plus measurement error. The UI caveats every BIA value.
4. **Body mass** uses an inertial (acceleration-based) device, because scales don't work in microgravity.
5. **Coefficients linking drivers to signals** were chosen to produce realistic directions and magnitudes for a demonstration. They are **not fitted to astronaut data**, which are not publicly available at this resolution.
6. **Reaction-time and memory tasks** are ORBITAL's own short tasks (PVT-inspired). Their scores are simulated.
7. **Skin temperature is not core temperature.** This is stated in the registry and in the UI.

## 4. Reproducibility
- All randomness is derived from `(profile seed, purpose label)` using FNV-1a hashing and mulberry32, so adding a new signal never changes existing numbers.
- `generateMission(id)` produces byte-identical output on every run (tested). Records have deterministic ids: `CM-0X:signal:day`.
- The app generates each mission once, stores it in SQLite (`records` table, about 19.6k rows) and reloads it on later launches. It regenerates only when `SIM_VERSION` changes. A test checks that the generate → persist → reload path gives identical snapshots and assessments.

## 5. Privacy in crew views
Crew-level views (Crew page, Overview crew strip, comparison chart and table) show **physical indicators and data completeness only**. Brain & Mental Health results and wellbeing self-reports appear only inside an individual's own profile. This is enforced in the main process: `crewSummary()` returns only physical signals, and a test asserts it.
