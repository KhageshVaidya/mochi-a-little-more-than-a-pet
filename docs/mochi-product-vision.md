# Mochi — A Little More Than a Pet · Product vision

Status: **proposal, awaiting approval.** No implementation has started. This document supersedes ORBITAL's *product* framing. ORBITAL's research, evaluation and decision records remain the historical record.

## 1. The idea in one paragraph

Mochi is a small friend who lives on an astronaut's computer. It sits on the desktop above other applications. It can be dragged, hidden and recalled, and it naps, blinks, wags its tail and plays. It talks briefly and naturally through a local language model, keeps a private journal *with* the astronaut when invited, and nudges them toward rest, play and time with the crew. It doesn't watch or lecture.

Behind it, **Dr G Zero** turns permitted data into deterministic, explainable assessments (SCND). **Fable** lets the crew's companions coordinate shared activities without sharing anyone's private information. The dashboard is secondary: something the astronaut opens, or asks Mochi to open, when they want the detail.

## 2. Three components

| Component | What it is to the astronaut | What it is technically |
|---|---|---|
| **Mochi** | "My little friend." Default companion; others are configurable | Desktop overlay window(s), character packages, conversation, journal and memory, local LLM client |
| **Dr G Zero** | "The detailed view of my health and mission." | SCND engine, synthetic data and signal processing, intelligence reports, recommendations, the dashboard |
| **Fable** | "Our companions help the crew get together." | Consent-gated coordination layer between companions: activity proposals, matching, crew-level aggregates |

## 3. Experience principles

1. **Mochi first.** After installation, the first thing that appears is Mochi on the desktop, not a dashboard. Everything social happens with Mochi. Dr G Zero is opened on request ("Mochi, show me my health").
2. **A friend, not a professional.** Short (usually 1–2 sentences, under 35 words), at most one question at a time, humour allowed, no lectures, no forced positivity, no theatrical stage directions. Mochi can tell when someone wants space and backs off.
3. **Invited, not imposed.** Journal questions are contextual and occasional, never a daily interrogation. Nothing is stored without the astronaut's control.
4. **Deterministic where it matters.** Health assessments, escalation and safety handling are deterministic and auditable. The language model only words things; it never decides.
5. **Private by default.** Journal, conversations and raw health data stay with the individual and on the device. Fable shares *intents and consent* (e.g. "open to a puzzle tonight"), never states.
6. **Play without pressure.** Rewards come from voluntary participation, never from body values. Rest counts, nothing is ever lost, there are no health leaderboards, and safety always overrides gamification.
7. **Honest about what is real.** Synthetic data is always labelled. If the local model isn't available, Mochi says so and uses clearly marked simple replies. It never pretends.

## 4. What Mochi is not

Not a medical device, therapist or diagnostic tool. Not a surveillance system. Not a chatbot hidden inside an app. Not a motivational poster.

## 5. A day with Mochi (target experience)

- **Morning:** Mochi stretches on the desktop corner. "Morning. Sleep okay?" It asks only if the astronaut hasn't been asked recently and didn't decline.
- **Mid-day:** a small bubble appears without stealing focus: "Maya and Daniel are up for a puzzle at 19:00. Want in?" This comes from Fable and uses only opt-in statuses.
- **Evening:** "I had a terrible day." → "Oof. Long day?" → … → "Fine. Want to beat me at a memory game?"
- **Any time:** "Open Dr G Zero" or "What changed this week?" opens the dashboard at the right page.
- **If someone asks for help:** a fixed, serious safety reply and the human-support pathway. This is never generated by the model and never playful.

## 6. Dashboard direction (Dr G Zero)

The reference artifact ("BIORBIT") shows the qualities we want. We'll borrow the qualities, not the design:
- **Calm surface:** one dark-first canvas with large-radius panels, a condensed display face for numbers and headings, and a plain body face.
- **Status language:** one four-level vocabulary used everywhere (*Nominal · Watch · Act soon · Urgent*), always with an icon and a label, never colour alone.
- **Charts that explain themselves:** a personal-baseline band, a "what this means" line, and "show data as table" for every chart.
- **Recommendations that state their basis:** "Based on: …", with Add to my plan / Done / Undo.
- **A persistent, quiet assistant entry point:** in our case, Mochi itself.
- **Explicit private areas:** mental wellbeing and journal are private, with an "on board, never shared" note.

The home screen shows eight things only:
1. crew overview
2. mission timeline
3. current wellbeing status
4. SCND summary
5. active recommendations
6. data completeness
7. alerts needing attention
8. crew activity and shared missions

Everything else is one click deeper.

## 7. Naming and provenance policy

- **Public product name:** Mochi — A Little More Than a Pet.
- **Dashboard and intelligence:** Dr G Zero.
- **Coordination layer:** Fable.
- **"ORBITAL":** removed from all user-facing text and the build. It is kept where it records history: research review, decision records D-001 to D-023, phase evaluation reports, and commit messages. A short note at the top of those documents will explain the rename.

## 8. Demonstration profiles

The public build uses five **original** fictional crew members (Alex Morgan, Maya Chen, Daniel Reed, Sofia Patel, James Wilson) and original companion artwork (Mochi plus original pets). Profiles and companions are data (character manifests), so a team can relabel them for a private local demonstration in a git-ignored configuration file that is never packaged or published. No third-party characters, names or artwork are distributed.

## 9. Success for the Space Apps demo

1. Someone installs Mochi on a Mac (and, once tested, Windows x86_64).
2. A first-run check finds or guides installation of Ollama and Qwen3:4B.
3. Mochi appears and chats through the real local model.
4. "Open Dr G Zero" shows a clean dashboard with an explainable SCND report on synthetic data.
5. Switching profiles shows a different astronaut, companion and history.
6. Fable demonstrates two companions coordinating a shared activity using only consented information.
